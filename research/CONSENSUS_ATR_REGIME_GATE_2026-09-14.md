# Consensus ATR regime gate research — 2026-09-14

Research-only. Production main, Stable★6, Monster, Core, batch02 shadow, Discord, and Spreadsheet workflows are untouched.

## Why this lane exists

The uncapped independent 3-head Consensus architecture is strongly positive in 2025 but reverses in 2026:

- V43 2025 min95: n=112, mean +6.35%, median +4.67%, win 65.18%.
- V42 2026 min95: n=69, mean -7.05%, median -7.57%, win 14.49%.
- V43 2025 min98: n=55, mean +10.23%.
- V42 2026 min98: n=47, mean -9.57%.

A static higher consensus threshold does not solve the failure.

## Important model audit

The classifier uses balanced sample weights. Therefore `p_win` and `p_hit10` are useful model scores/ranking inputs but are **not calibrated probabilities**. A value such as `p_hit10=0.8` must not be read as an 80% empirical probability.

Within the already selected candidates, rank relationship also changes materially:

- 2025 min95 Spearman(`p_hit10`, 5BD return): about +0.33.
- 2026 min95: about -0.28.
- 2025 min95 Spearman(`pred_ret`, 5BD return): about +0.23.
- 2026 min95: about -0.29.
- 2025 min95 Spearman(`cons_min`, 5BD return): about +0.39.
- 2026 min95: about -0.34.

Interpretation: 2026 is not merely weaker calibration; the ranking relationship itself is partly inverted.

## Low-DOF pre-2026 regime guard

Use only one contemporaneously observable market variable already present in the feature set:

`market_median_atr = median(ATR14_pct) across the eligible date/session candidate universe`.

ATR14_pct is defined as 14-day ATR / close * 100.

A deliberately simple distribution guard was frozen from **2025-01-01 through 2025-06-30 only**:

- base selector: fixed min95 Consensus
- rule: allow Consensus only when `market_median_atr <= q90(reference)`
- frozen q90 threshold: **2.8640659721**

This q90 is a distribution guard, not a threshold optimized on 2026 outcomes.

## New audit: rolling q90 is unsafe

The originally proposed next step was a fully causal rolling reference:
`current ATR <= q90(all previous ATR contexts)`.

This is causal but **fails economically**.

As persistent high-ATR 2026 observations enter the history, the q90 threshold drifts upward:
- starts around 2.8569 at the 2026 boundary;
- expands above 3.0 quickly;
- reaches roughly 3.63 in the expanding version;
- 30/60-session rolling versions reach roughly 3.74-3.78.

The expanding-q90 min95 policy then reopens 48/69 2026 trades with mean about **-7.58%**.
Rolling windows reopen 57-61 trades with means around **-7.0% to -7.3%**.

Concrete failure:
- early June 2026 is correctly vetoed while ATR is around 3.6-3.9 and the historical threshold is around 2.86-3.0;
- after only a few weeks, the reference distribution normalizes to the bad high-volatility state;
- by 2026-06-17 the expanding threshold is already around 3.61 and bad-regime trades begin to pass again.

Conclusion: a rolling percentile is not an OOD detector once it learns the shifted regime.

## Revised decision

Treat the ATR rule as a **frozen pre-deployment calibration-distribution OOD circuit breaker**, not an adaptive market-timing filter.

Recommended research specification:
1. Consensus min95 remains the candidate selector.
2. At model-freeze time, compute the q90 of `market_median_atr` from the frozen pre-deployment reference cohort.
3. Store that ATR cap inside the model/version manifest.
4. Do **not** update the cap automatically from live high-volatility data.
5. When current market median ATR exceeds the frozen cap, Consensus emits NO TRADE.
6. Re-estimating the reference distribution requires a new explicitly versioned model/research cycle; it is not an online adaptation.

This is consistent with the observed failure mechanism: 2026 is a covariate-shift/OOD state in which the Consensus ranking relationship becomes partly inverted.

## What this solves

- preserves the strong 2025 Consensus regime;
- blocks the known 2026 high-volatility regime collapse without tuning the original q90 on 2026 outcomes;
- uses one simple, observable, TV-free market statistic;
- prevents dangerous online normalization to a bad regime;
- provides a natural arbitration boundary with independent Monster/Core lanes.

## Remaining limitations

- min95+ produces no trades in the tested 2026 high-volatility window, so another lane must supply opportunities there or the system must accept NO TRADE;
- the ATR reference distribution is currently estimated from selected-candidate date/session artifacts, not all eligible market contexts;
- a future implementation should freeze the reference from the complete eligible date/session universe where possible;
- execution timing and survivorship constraints remain;
- 2026 is already inspected and is robustness evidence, not pristine OOS.

## Next non-overlapping research

1. Do not spend more degrees of freedom tuning the ATR cutoff.
2. Specify the frozen-ATR OOD gate as a model-manifest contract.
3. Audit whether the same frozen cap works when computed from the full eligible context universe rather than only selected min95 contexts.
4. When parallel Monster/Core full-JPX evidence is available, evaluate **arbitration**, not blended retraining:
   - normal-volatility/in-distribution -> Consensus;
   - high-volatility/OOD -> Monster/Core if that lane independently passes, otherwise NO TRADE.
5. Keep production unchanged until prospective/shadow evidence exists.


## Terminology correction: calibration distribution, not literal model-training distribution

The 2.8640659721 cap was computed from Jan-Jun 2025 min95-selected market contexts. It did not use 2026 outcomes and did not optimize a return threshold, but Jan-Jun 2025 is **not identical to the row set used to fit every prequential model**.

Therefore:
- call the source a frozen **pre-deployment calibration/reference distribution**;
- do not claim the value is the q90 of the exact model-training feature distribution;
- V45 will additionally report the full-context Oct-Dec 2024 initial-history ATR distribution and the full-context Jan-Jun 2025 calibration distribution, without strategy outcomes;
- no threshold is changed from this terminology correction.
